India has successfully landed the Chandrayaan 3 on the moon. Let's read to find out more about India's moon landing mission.

Chandeayaan 3 is India's third exploration mission to the Moon. It was designed and executed by ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation). It consists of a lander called Vikram and a rover called Pragyan. The mission was to achieve a soft landing on the surface of the moon, drive a rover of the surface of the moon and conduct experiments on the moon to understand its composition.

Chandrayaan 3 was launched on 14 July 2023 from the Satish Dhawan Space Centere Second Launch Pad in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh. It landed on the south pole of the moon at 6:04p.m. on 23 August 2023. India is the first country in the world to land on the rough terrian of the south pole of the moon. It is now the fourth country to soft land on the Moon.

Imagine a spacecraft moving through space at ten times the speed of an aeoroplane. It has to come atmost to a standstill before landing on the Moon. The speed has to be reduce in a matter of minutes any human to put the brakes on. Sounds difficult, does it not? This is exactly what the scientists at ISRO archieved when the Chandrayaan 3 landed softly on the moon.

Chandrayaan 3 consisted of 3 main components:

Propulsion module

It carried the lander and rover to the lunar orbit.

Lander

The lander, Vikram is a spacecraft designed in the shape of a box with four legs and four landing thrusters. It carried the rover, Pragyan and other scientific instruments to perform on-site experiments.

Rover

The Pragyan is a six-wheeled vehicle. Its job is to take measurements to provide data for resarch about the composotion of the Moon's surface, the presence of water ice in the soil and the evolution of the Moon's atmosphere.

The propulsion moudule was instered into the lunar orbit on 5 August 2023. The lander seperated from the propulsion moudule on 17 August and began its final phrase to descend on the Moon.

The Vikram lander hovered above the Moon's surface at a distance of 30 kilometers at a speed of 1.6 kilometers/second.

In order to archive a soft landing on the chosen spot, its engines were ignited so that its speed could be reduced. This speed reduction combined with the gravity of the Moon guited the Vikram Lander at a speed of 60meters/second.
