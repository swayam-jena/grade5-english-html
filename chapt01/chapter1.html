Off on a World Tour

What to Know

This chapter is an extract from the famous novel Around the word in Eighty Days by the French writer, Jules Verne, published in 1872. It is about two men, Phileas Fogg, an Englishman, and his friend and butler, Jean Paaseparout (pronounced jean pas-par-too) and the adventures they have as thay race to travel around the world in eighty days.

Listen, Read and Understand

We are going to read the introduction to the two main characters in the novel, Around the World in Eighty days. Let us read to find out what they are like.

Mr. Phelias Fogg lived, in 1872, at No .7, Savile Row, Burlington Gardens. He was one of the most important members of the Reform Club, though he didn't like attracting attention.

He was an Englishman, but probably not a Londerner. Nobody knew what his job was or how he spent his time. He was a secretive man. Phileas Fogg was a member of the Reform Club, for which he was recommended by the Barings.

Was Phlieas Fogg rich? Yes. But nobody knew where his money came from. He talked very little and was very mysterious. Had he travelled? Probably, because he knew a lot of about the world.

Phlieas Fogg had not left London for many years. His only pastimes were reading the newspapers and playing card games. He didn't have a wife or children. He didn't have any relatives or freinds either, which is very unusual.

He lived alone in his house, which nobody entered except his one butler. He ate his meals at the club every day, at the same table, and always alone. He went home at midnight every night and went to bed straight away.

The mansion in Savile Row was confortable. Mr Fogg Didn't ask for much but he was very particular about details. That morning he dismissed his butler for bringing some water to shave which was a little too cool. Now he was waiting for a new butler to arrive.

Phleias Fogg sat in his armchair, his hands on his knees, his body straight, watching the clock. At exactly half past-eleven Mr. Fogg would according to his daily habit, leave Savile Row, an go to his club.

A knock as this moment soundend on the door and the dismisses butler, appeared.

'You are four minutes too slow. No matter; it's enough to mention the error. Now from this moment, twenty-nine minutes after eleven a.m., this Wednesday, 2nd October, you would for me.'

Phlieas Fogg got up, took his hat in has left hand, put it on his head, and went off without a word.
