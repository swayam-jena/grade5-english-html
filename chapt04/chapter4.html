Grandpa Fights an Ostrich

What to Know

Ruskin Bond is a popular Indian author who has written many books for children. Many of his books have been made into films around the world. 'Grandpa Fights an Ostrich' is a story from a collection of humorous animal stories for children.

Listen, Read and Understand

We are going to read a humorous story about a man's emcounter with an ostrich. Let us read and find out what happens to the man and how he manages to escape from a dangerous situation.

Before Grandfather joined the Indian Railways, he worked for a few years on the East African Railways. It was during the period that he had his famous encounter with the ostrich.

This is the story on his own words.

While engaged in the laying of a new railway line, I had a miraculous escape from an awful death.

I lived in small township, but my work lay some twelve miles away and I had to go to the work site and back on horseback.

One day my horse had a slight accident. I decided to do the journey on foot, as I was a great walker in those days. I knew of a shortcut through the hills that would save me about six miles.

This shortcut went through an ostrich farm, or 'camp', as it was called. It was breeding season. At this time, the male birds are very aggresive and are ready to attack at the slightest provocation. But I was fairly familiar with the ways of ostriches and I knew my dog would scare away any bird that might try to attack me.

Strange through it may seem, the biggest ostriches, (some of them grow to a height of nine feet!) can run faster than a racehorse at the slight of even a small dog. And so, I felt quite safe in the company of Sam, the moregel who had adopted me some two monts previously.

On arrival at the camp, I got through the wire fencing. Keeping a good lookout, I dodged across the spaces between teh thorn bushes. Now and then I got a glimpse of the birds freeding some distance away.

I had gone about half a mile from the fence when out from the bushes bolted a hare. In an instant, Sam gave chase. I tried calling him back, through I knew this was hopeless as chasing hares was a passion with him.

Whether it was the dog's bark or my own shouting, I don't know, but just what I was most anxious to avoid, immediately happenend. The ostriches were startled and began darting to and fro.

Suddenly I saw a big male bird emerge from a large shrub about hundred yards away. He stared at me for a few moments. Then, with his short wings spread and tail erect, he came bounding towards me.

I had nothing, not even a stick, wiht which to defend myself. I turned and ran for the fence.

But it was an unequal race. What were my steps of two or feet against the creature's great strides of sixteen or twenty? there was only one hope: to get behind a large bush and try to hide from the bird until help came. And so, I rushed for the nearest clump of thorn bushes.

The great bird was almost immediately upon me. This way and that I dodged behind the bushes, talking to great care that I did not get directly in front of the ostrich's deadly kick.

Breathless, and really quite helpless, I called wildly for help. I knew it would soon be immposible to keep going; I was ready to drop from exhausion.

As if aware of my condition, the infuriated bird suddenly doubled back on his course and charged straight at me. With a desperate effort, I managed to step to one side.

How it happened I don't know, but I found myself holding on to one of the creature's wings, quite close to its body. I don't know which of us was more suprised.

It was now the bird's turn to be frightened. He began to turn round and round in a sort of dance, moving so quickly that my feet were soon swinging out almost horizontally! All the time the ostrich kept opening and shutting his break wiht loud snaps.

My arms soon began to ache with the strain, and the swift and continous circling was making me dizzy. But I knew that if I relaxed my hold, a terrible fate awaited me. it seemed as if that spiteful bird would never tire. And I could not hold on much longer.

Suddenly, the ostrich went into reverse. This unexpected move not only had the effect of making me lose my hold but also sent me sprawling to the ground. I landed in a heap near the thorn bushes.

In an instant, almost before I had time to realise what had happened, the big bird loomed over me. I thought the end had come. Instinctively, I raised my hands to protect my face. But to my suprise, the ostrich did not strike.

I parted my hands and peered up. There stood the creature with one foot raised, ready to deliver that deadly kick! I coudn't move. Was the bird going to play cat-and-mouse with me and prolong the agony?

As I watched, fascinated, the ostrich looked sharply to the left. A second later, he jumped back, turned, and took off as fast as he could go.

Dazed I wondered what had happened to make him beat an unexpected retreat.

I soon found out. To my great joy, I heard the bark of my dog, and the next moment he was jumping around me, licking my face and hands.

Needless to say, I took good care to see that he did not leave my side again until we were well out of the ostrich camp.
