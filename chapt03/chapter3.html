A Mouses's Tale

What to Know

This poem has been taken from the book Alice adventures in Wonderland written by Lewis Caroll and published in 1865. In this book Alice, a little girl, falls down a rabbit hole and meets some faboulous creatures in an underground world. She meets a Hatter, a Kare and Queen of Hearts. She has many adventures and wakes up just in time for tea as the book ends.

Listen, Read and Understand

Alice and the animals of wonderland lave all become wat as they were swimming in a pool of Alice's tears. Now, they want to get dry. The mouse tired to tell them a dull story but the Dodo suggested a Cactus-race. It is a race where everybody runs whereever they want and they can start and stop running at any time. The Mouse, when the race ended, told his story about why he did not like the Cat and the Dog. Let us read the poem as Alice remembers it.

Fury said to the mouse, That he met in the house, 'Let us both go to the law: I wil prosecute you.-Come, I'll take no denail; We must have a trial; For really this morning I've nothing to do.' Said the mouse to the cur, 'Such a trial, dear sir, With no jury or judge, would be wasting our breath.' 'I'll be judge, I'll be jury,' Said cunning old Fury; 'I'll try the whole cause, and condemn you to dealth.'
